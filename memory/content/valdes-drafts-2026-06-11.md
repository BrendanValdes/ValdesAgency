## Draft 2 — S7 LinkedIn long-form — LinkedIn [TEXT]
Voice-check: PASS · soft flag: "says 2009" (deliberate, keep)

Spent the last few weeks auditing pool companies across the Las Vegas Valley. Henderson, Spring Valley, North Las Vegas, Paradise, and Las Vegas itself. Five audits. Same pattern showed up in all five.

Every single one had a trust problem. Not a marketing problem. A trust problem.

One company had 62 Yelp reviews. Exceptional for this market. But the email on their site was a Yahoo address. The homeowner who found them did the math instantly. 62 reviews says real company. Yahoo email says 2009.

Another company was a Top Pro on Thumbtack. Paying $25 to $60 per lead. No website. So every lead they bought had nowhere to land. The Thumbtack profile did all the trust-building. Then the homeowner searched the company name and found nothing. Deal gone.

One site still had a different business name in the footer. Not their business. A completely different company from a completely different state.

Here is the pattern nobody is talking about.

Pool companies pour money into lead generation. Thumbtack, Yelp, HomeAdvisor. They fight hard for visibility. Then they hand the homeowner three separate reasons not to trust them before the first call happens.

The leads are not the problem. The trust infrastructure is.

A homeowner in Henderson does not care how many platforms you are on. They care whether you look like a real business when they check. And they always check. They Google the name. They look at the website. They read the email address. They notice whether Google has any reviews.

If one of those things looks wrong, they move to the next company. No phone call. No message. Just gone.

30 seconds and they are out.

The fix is not a new marketing channel. The fix is making sure the business looks legitimate everywhere a homeowner might land. Consistent name. A real domain email. Google reviews that match the Yelp reviews. A website that is clearly about this business.

None of that is complicated. All of it gets ignored.

Pool companies in this market are losing warm leads they already paid for. Not because the leads were bad. Because the trust check failed after the lead clicked.

Fix the trust infrastructure first. Then spend on leads.
