## Why This Script Works

V2 layered five sales frameworks over V1 and still pitched in the opener. V3 strips it down to NEPQ basics: disarm, pull current state, pull pain, pull goals, close on the gap. Five sections, each with one job. Pain shows up in Section 3, goals in Section 4. The contrast between where they are and where they want to be IS the sale. Section 5 close points at that gap in the prospect's own words. No magic question. No dollar math. No scarcity.

The differentiator: nine inline tonality reads at the three moments where reading the prospect's voice changes the move (disarm, pain dive, close). Default delivery is calm, curious, slow, one notch lower-status than the prospect. Rushed prospects get a tighter version. Annoyed prospects get a disarm and an exit option.

## Pre-Call Read (first 3 seconds, pick one)

1. **RUSHED:** clipped "yeah?", background noise, sigh, "make it quick"
2. **CALM:** relaxed "hello," lets silence sit, no background noise
3. **ANNOYED:** flat "what," "who is this," interruption inside 3 seconds

Default delivery is built for CALM. Sections 1, 3, 5 have explicit deviations for the other two.

## SECTION 1: DISARM (5 seconds)

```
"Hey... is this [Name]?"

[Wait for confirmation. Slow.]

"Cool. Brendan, calling out of Vegas. I was wondering if you could
help me out real quick."

[Pause. Let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]
```

### [PERCEPTION CALIBRATION | DISARM]

**[READ 1: RUSHED]**
Tighten to: *"Brendan out of Vegas, real quick, could you help me out for a sec?"* If they cut you off, offer callback immediately: *"Yeah no, you sound slammed. Better to catch you later today or tomorrow morning?"* Get a real time. Don't push.

**[READ 2: CALM]**
Default delivery. Slow it further. Two full seconds after "Cool." Let the name land. Calm prospects reward patience. Don't bring energy.

**[READ 3: ANNOYED]**
Disarm first: *"Yeah no, I get it. This is random. Not selling you anything. Just had one question about how pool companies in town are getting new customers. If it's a bad time I'll get out of your way."* If they soften, continue. If they double down, exit clean: *"Totally fair. Appreciate the honesty. Have a good one."*

### [BRIDGE → SECTION 2]
- "Cool, appreciate that." (if they said yes)
- "Yeah no problem." (if they hesitated and softened)

## SECTION 3: PAIN DIVE (pick ONE option based on Section 2 answer)

Don't ask all of these. Pick one based on what they actually said. If their answer was small, ask a second pain question to deepen. Never more than two or three questions total in this section.

```
OPTION A | if they said referrals or word of mouth only:
"When referrals slow down or dry up, what does that usually look
like for the business?"

OPTION B | if they named online, Google, Facebook, Yelp:
"And when leads come in from there, where do you feel like they're
slipping through?"

OPTION C | if they sounded confident or said "things are good":
"Have you noticed any spots where stuff isn't working as well as
you'd like? Even small stuff."

OPTION D | universal feeling check, use if A/B/C don't fit:
"How are you feeling about how it's been going lately?"

OPTION E | the fix-one-thing question, use late in Section 3 to
sharpen the pain:
"If you could fix one thing about how you're getting customers
right now, what would it be?"
```

### [PERCEPTION CALIBRATION | PAIN DIVE]

**[READ 1: RUSHED]**
Shrink the question. Drop softeners. *"Real quick, anywhere in there leads slipping through?"* If they say no, don't push. Pivot to: *"Got it. Mind if I follow up in a couple days when you've got more time?"* Get permission for callback.

**[READ 2: CALM]**
Default. After they answer, hold silence for three to four seconds before bridging. Calm prospects often add the most important sentence AFTER you'd normally bridge. Wait for it.

**[READ 3: ANNOYED]**
If they answered Section 1 but stayed clipped, shrink to a yes/no: *"Real quick, is referrals what you're leaning on most these days?"* Yes or no buys you the next question. Open-ended dies on annoyed prospects.

### [BRIDGE → SECTION 4]
- "Right, yeah."
- "Yeah that's actually pretty common."
- "Hmm, yeah."


> "But based on what you just said, sounds like there's a gap between leads slipping through when things get busy, and you wanting to be in a spot where you stop even thinking about it. Would you be open to a quick fifteen minutes this week to look at what's actually causing that gap?"

### [PERCEPTION CALIBRATION | CLOSE]

**[READ 1: RUSHED]**
Tighten: *"Real quick, would you be open to fifteen minutes this week to look at what's causing that gap? I can put it together on my time, you just tell me when."* "I do the work, you pick the time" lowers the ask.

**[READ 2: CALM]**
Default delivery. Hold silence. They're weighing. Don't refill. If hesitation, soften: *"Take your time, no rush."*

**[READ 3: ANNOYED]**
Don't try the full close. Pivot to callback ask: *"Honestly, this doesn't sound like the right moment. Would it be alright if I followed up in a couple weeks when things are calmer?"* Get permission. That's the win.

## NEPQ Objection Handles

Acknowledge briefly. Ask a question that splits the objection. Let them resolve it.

### "Not interested."

```
"Yeah no, totally fair. Real quick before I let you go, what makes
you say that, is it that what you're doing now is already working
well, or is it that this kind of thing hasn't worked for you in the
past?"

[Wait. Don't defend.]

If "already working": "Got it, that's the dream. What's working best
for you right now?" (back into situation Q)
If "hasn't worked": "Yeah, most common thing I hear. What happened
the last time?"
```

### "Already have someone."

```
"Got it. Are they handling all of it or just certain pieces?"

[Silence. They name the gap.]

"Yeah that tracks. And the parts they're handling, is that actually
moving the needle for you or is it more just kind of there?"
```

### "Send me info."

```
"Yeah I get that. Real quick, what would you actually want to see
in something I sent? Just so I'm not wasting your time with the
wrong stuff."

[Wait. They describe what would be useful.]

"Got it, makes sense. Honestly that's the kind of thing that's hard
to put in an email without me knowing more about your setup. Would
you be open to a quick fifteen so I can pull it together for you
specifically?"
```

### "Want to think about it."

```
"Yeah, totally. Real quick though, what specifically are you
thinking about, is it whether it'd actually help, or the timing, or
something else?"

[Wait. "Think about it" is never the real objection.]

If "whether it'd help": "Got it. What would you need to see to feel
like it would?"
If "timing": "Yeah. When does timing usually clear up for you?"
If "something else": let them name it, then question into it.
```

### "How much?"

```
"Honest answer, depends on what you actually need, and I don't know
that yet. Real quick though, before I throw a number that's
probably wrong, what would have to be true for the cost to be worth
it for you?"

[Wait. Long pause is fine.]

"Right, yeah, makes sense. That's actually what the quick fifteen
would be for, figuring out what 'worth it' looks like on your side.
Worth a look?"
```

## Recovery Moves

Six scenarios. Acknowledge + reset tone + smaller re-engagement.

**1. Came in too hot and they're calm.**
Catch it inside 10 seconds. Slow down mid-sentence. Drop volume by 20%. *"Sorry, let me start that over."* Restart at half speed.

**2. Came in too calm and they want to move fast.**
Tighten cadence by 30%. Skip softeners. *"Yeah no, I'll be quick. One question."* Go straight to a closed-form version of the situation Q.

**3. Stumbled on a word.**
Quick recover: *"Sorry, let me reset."* Pause one beat. Restart cleanly. Acknowledged stumbles read human; ignored ones read anxious.

**4. They interrupt mid-question.**
Stop talking immediately. Listen all the way through. Bridge: *"Got it, yeah."* Then ask: *"Mind if I finish what I was asking? Should take 10 seconds."*

**5. One-word answer to an open question.**
Don't move on. *"Got it. What does that look like for you day to day?"* Or: *"Yeah? Tell me more about that."* "Tell me more" opens about 70% of one-word answers. If they go one-word twice in a row, the call is dead. Exit clean: *"Sounds like maybe not the right time. Appreciate you taking the call."*

**6. Long silence after a question (4+ seconds).**
Don't fill it. If it crosses 5-6 seconds, soften: *"Take your time, no rush."* If they still don't answer, shrink to a closed question. Trade open for closed; that's the trade you wanted.

## Bridge Phrase Library

Use liberally. Without bridges, NEPQ is a survey.

- "Got it."
- "Makes sense."
- "Yeah that tracks."
- "Right, yeah."
- "Hmm, yeah."
- "Interesting."
- "Fair enough."
- "Tell me more about that."
- "Yeah that makes sense."

Mix them. Repeating the same one three times in a row reads robotic.
