# Las Vegas Luxury Pool Service: Competitive Landscape & Where Melton Stands

**Prepared for:** Christian Melton, Melton Pool Management
**Date:** June 6, 2026
**Scope:** The luxury pool-service market across your four target communities (Anthem, Seven Hills, MacDonald Ranch/Highlands, Summerlin), plus an honest look at where Melton currently shows up versus the field.

*All ratings below are labeled by their source (Yelp, Google, HomeAdvisor, etc.) because they are not interchangeable. Everything here comes from public sources anyone, including your customers, can see.*

---

## The one-page takeaway

The luxury pool market in Las Vegas is fragmented and soft. No single company owns it. The shops that *call* themselves luxury each have a real hole. One has no reviews at all. One is a corporate roll-up its own customers complain about. One is headquartered on the wrong side of town with a typo in its phone number.

Meanwhile, the single richest gated community is unclaimed. Not one pool company runs a dedicated page for MacDonald Ranch/Highlands.

Your reputation is genuinely strong: 4.2★ on Google, a perfect 5.0★ on HomeAdvisor, A+ with the BBB. The gap isn't your work or your reviews. It's that none of it shows up where a luxury homeowner actually searches, and your own website has a few leaks quietly working against you.

## The luxury field, company by company

### Two Brothers Luxury Pool Service: the most direct name competitor
- **Where they serve:** dedicated pages for Anthem and Seven Hills. MacDonald Ranch gets a brief mention. No Summerlin.
- **Pricing:** published, roughly $79–$149/month depending on tier. One of the few that shows prices.
- **Services:** full weekly service (a "23-step" process they promote), remodels, repairs, new builds, salt chemistry, algae-free guarantee.
- **The hole:** their Yelp listing has zero reviews and is unclaimed. For a company branding itself as *the* luxury name, there's no proof behind it. The website runs on a generic pool-industry template used by many companies, and the same three testimonials repeat on every page. No owner is named anywhere.

### Squeaky Clean Pools: best community coverage
- **Where they serve:** dedicated pages for Anthem, Seven Hills, and Summerlin (two pages), but no MacDonald Ranch page. Veteran-owned (owner: Juan).
- **Pricing:** not published.
- **Services:** maintenance, repairs, green-to-clean, drains. No salt conversions, remodels, or automation.
- **The hole:** a perfect 5.0★ on Yelp, but only 7 reviews. Strong coverage, very thin proof, and a narrower service menu than yours.

### Splash / SPS PoolCare: the cautionary tale
- **Where they serve:** broad valley-wide coverage with text mentions of MacDonald Ranch, Anthem, and Summerlin, but no dedicated community pages.
- **Pricing:** published, "as low as $169/month." The highest entry price in the group.
- **The hole:** the original local Splash was acquired by a large national roll-up. Its Yelp sits at 3.8★ across 255 reviews, the lowest rating in the luxury group. Customers publicly complain (including on Reddit) that service got worse after the company went corporate. The website is a national template with no local feel.

### Designer Pool Services: ranks for the keyword, wrong neighborhood
- **Where they serve:** headquartered in northwest Las Vegas (Centennial Hills), far from the Henderson luxury belt. Summerlin appears only in their social posts. No dedicated community pages.
- **Pricing:** not published.
- **Services:** cleaning, restoration, repairs, saltwater conversions, remodels, certified operators.
- **The hole:** their website lists two different phone numbers, one with a Colorado area code. That typo quietly hurts trust and search. The site is a Wix build using stock photos rather than real customer pools, and 22 Yelp reviews is thin for a company claiming the #1 luxury spot.

### Summerlin Hills Pool Service: Summerlin only
- **Where they serve:** Summerlin and the northwest exclusively. No east-side luxury coverage (owner: Mike).
- **Pricing:** not published.
- **The hole:** a solid 4.7★ on Yelp (24 reviews). But every testimonial on the website is from 2015, repairs are referred out to other vendors rather than handled in-house, and there's no social presence.

### Luxent Pools: great looking, no proof
- **Where they serve:** lists Summerlin among a broad area. No dedicated community pages. Uses a Gmail address.
- **The hole:** the best-looking website in the group (modern, with a real before/after gallery and luxury imagery). But it's backed by no Yelp listing and 123 Instagram followers. It markets the luxury *look* without the proof. This one is worth noting, because it's the inverse of your situation. You have the proof they lack. You just don't yet have the presentation they have.

### Jonathan's Pool Solutions: the technical benchmark
- **Where they serve:** lists Seven Hills, Henderson, Summerlin and more. Anthem not named. No dedicated community pages.
- **Pricing:** not published. Customers describe it as "fair" and "reasonable."
- **Services:** the deepest technical menu in the group. Salt systems, heaters, full automation (Pentair), equipment overhauls.
- **The hole:** 4.8★ on Yelp (68 reviews), the strongest rating-and-volume combo in the field. But he markets himself as a value-priced repair pro, not a luxury service, and runs no community-targeted pages. His website looks plainer than the quality of his actual work.

### Lake Las Vegas Pools: geographic specialist
- **Where they serve:** Lake Las Vegas and Henderson. No dedicated community pages despite being near MacDonald Highlands.
- **Services:** the broadest scope. Full service plus remodels, new construction, spas, water features.
- **The hole:** 4.2★ on Yelp with only 9 reviews after four years, including a couple of pointed complaints (a billing/damage dispute and a missed appointment). The website is a dated template with placeholder text still visible.

## Where Melton stands today

**Your genuine strengths. You're ahead of much of the field here:**
- **Google 4.2★ / 21 reviews** plus a perfect **HomeAdvisor 5.0★ / 11 reviews.** That's more real proof than Two Brothers (0 reviews), Squeaky Clean (7), Lake Las Vegas (9), or Luxent (none).
- **A+ BBB accreditation** since 2022.
- An **owner-does-the-work reputation** with documented same-day and Sunday emergency response, often within 10 to 30 minutes. Only one competitor even attempts this, and they refer their repairs out.
- Real technical depth (salt, automation, equipment) that matches the strongest operator in the field.

**The gaps quietly costing you visibility and trust:**
- **No community pages.** Every competitor has at least some dedicated-page presence in these zips. Melton has none. So on a search like "pool service MacDonald Ranch," you don't appear, even though that community is unclaimed.
- **A broken link on your own site.** The "Learn More" button on your Gallery page currently sends visitors to your web designer's demo site instead of your contact page.
- **A dead second domain.** `meltonpoolmanagement.com` no longer loads. But it's still listed as your official website on the BBB, MapQuest, and HomeAdvisor. People clicking from those directories hit a dead end.
- **A Gmail address** as your public business email. A small thing that high-end buyers notice.
- **Your Yelp (3.2★)** is being dragged down by a handful of 1-star reviews that other customers have publicly flagged as looking coordinated. The underlying service quality reads far higher. The profile just isn't being managed or disputed.
- **Your Google reviews aren't claimed** on the aggregator showing them. And none of your 30-plus positive reviews appear on your own website.
- **Your photos show equipment and repairs, not finished luxury pools.** The high-value homes you work on aren't being shown the way a luxury buyer wants to see them.
- **Two business addresses** appear across directories (Henderson vs. Boulder City). That splits your local-search authority.

## The opportunities, in plain order

1. **Claim the open ground.** Build dedicated pages for Anthem, Seven Hills, MacDonald Ranch, and Summerlin. MacDonald Ranch in particular is unclaimed by anyone. First mover wins it.
2. **Fix the quiet leaks.** Repair the broken Gallery link, sort out the dead second domain still listed on directories, move to a branded email, and unify your address across platforms. Small, fast, and they're working against you right now.
3. **Put your proof to work.** Claim your Google review profile, add your real testimonials to your website, and respond to (and dispute) the questionable Yelp reviews. The proof already exists. It's just not being shown or defended.
4. **Lead with what no one else markets.** Your owner-does-it, Sunday-emergency, fast-response reputation is your sharpest edge. Right now it's buried in your reviews instead of on your homepage.
5. **Show the work like the luxury work it is.** The high-end homes you already service should be photographed and presented the way buyers in these communities expect. Then actually distributed where they'll see them.

**The short version:** you already have the reputation the loudest "luxury" competitors are faking. The opportunity is to show up where those buyers search, present the work at the level it deserves, and claim the communities nobody else has bothered to.
