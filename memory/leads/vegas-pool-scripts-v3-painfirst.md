# 📞 Vegas Pool Cold Call Script: V3 Pain-First

**Callback number:** 702-523-8826
**Default tone:** calm, curious, slow, one notch lower-status than the prospect
**Variant of:** V3-Simplified (memory/leads/vegas-pool-scripts-v3-simplified.md)
**Key difference:** Section 2 leads with pain hypothesis (2-3 specific pains from a library of 7), not an open situation question. Pain-first gets to the meat in 30 seconds instead of 90.

---

## ⏱️ Pre-Call Read (first 3 seconds)

| Tone    | Cue                                                          |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| RUSHED  | Clipped "yeah?", background noise, sigh, "make it quick"     |
| CALM    | Relaxed "hello," lets silence sit, no background noise       |
| ANNOYED | Flat "what," "who is this," interruption inside 3 seconds    |

Default delivery is built for CALM. Sections 1, 2, 3, 5 have deviations.

## 🚪 If It's Not the Owner (Gatekeeper)

"Hey, who would I talk to about how you guys are handling new customer inquiries on your end?"

**If not them, they're around:**

"Got it. They around right now? Or is there a better time to catch them?"

**If you need the name:**

"And what's their name so I know who to ask for?"

[Get the name. Hang up. Call back later asking for them by name. Do NOT pitch the gatekeeper.]

## 👋 SECTION 1: DISARM (5 seconds)

```
"Hey... is this [Name]?"

[Wait for confirmation. Slow.]

"Cool. Brendan, calling out of Vegas. I was wondering if you could
help me out real quick."

[Pause. Let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]
```

**Tonality reads:**

| Tone           | Adjustment                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RUSHED         | Tighten: *"Brendan out of Vegas, real quick, could you help me out for a sec?"* If cut off, offer callback immediately: *"Yeah no, you sound slammed. Better to catch you later today or tomorrow morning?"* Get a real time. Don't push. |
| CALM (default) | Slow further. Two full seconds after "Cool." Let the name land. Calm prospects reward patience. Don't bring energy.                                                                                                                     |
| ANNOYED        | Disarm: *"Yeah no, I get it. This is random. Not selling you anything. Just had one question about how pool companies in town are getting new customers. If it's a bad time I'll get out of your way."* If they double down, exit clean: *"Totally fair. Appreciate the honesty. Have a good one."* |

**Bridge → Section 2:** "Cool, appreciate that." (if yes) / "Yeah no problem." (if hesitated and softened)

## 🎯 SECTION 2: PAIN HYPOTHESIS (lead with 2-3 specific pains)

```
"So real quick, most pool guys I'm talking to right now are
running into one of a few things. Either [PAIN A], or [PAIN B],
or [PAIN C]. Is any of that hitting close to home for you, or is
it something else entirely?"
```

**Pain Library (pick 2-3 based on lead intel):**

- **A | Lead follow-up speed:** "leads come in but nobody's following up fast enough so they're going to whoever calls back first"
- **B | Referral dependence:** "running mostly on referrals and word of mouth and praying it doesn't dry up"
- **C | Website not converting:** "website's there but it's not really bringing in the kind of jobs they want"
- **D | Reviews not where they want them:** "customers are happy but they're not getting reviews unless they basically beg for them"
- **E | Seasonal swings hitting hard:** "busy seasons crush them and slow seasons feel scary"
- **F | Can't compete with bigger guys:** "the bigger pool companies in town keep taking the easy jobs and they're stuck competing on price"
- **G | Burnt by past marketing:** "tried marketing before, got burnt, don't really trust agencies"

**How to pick which 2-3 (check lead enrichment before dialing):**

- Outdated/template website → PAIN C is likely
- Under 40 reviews despite years in business → PAIN D
- Site mentions only premium service → PAIN F
- Solo operator or family business → PAIN B is likely
- Newer business under 5 years → PAIN A or B
- Default if unsure → A + B + C (covers most common scenarios)

**Listen for response:**

- ✅ They confirm one specifically → go to Section 3, use 🔬 probing phrase to deepen
- ✅ They correct you ("nah, mine is actually X") → even better, that's the real pain
- ❌ They deny all of them ("none of those, we're good") → fall back: *"Gotcha. So what are you guys doing right now that's working?"* (this is the v3-simplified situation question, used as fallback only)

**Bridges based on their response:**

- If confirmed: *"Yeah that's exactly what I hear from most guys. Tell me more about that."*
- If corrected: *"Got it, makes more sense."*
- If denied: *"Cool, no worries."*

**Tonality reads:**

| Tone           | Adjustment                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RUSHED         | Drop to 2 pains only. *"Most guys I'm talking to either can't follow up on leads fast enough or they're running on referrals. Either of those you?"* Yes/no buys the next question. |
| CALM (default) | 3 pains, full frame. Let them think. Don't refill silence.                                                                                                                |
| ANNOYED        | One pain only. *"Most pool guys right now are dealing with leads slipping through. That you, or is it something else?"* If denied, exit polite: *"Got it, sounds like you're in a good spot. Appreciate the time."* |

## 🔍 SECTION 3: PAIN DIVE (deepen what they confirmed)

After they confirm or correct in Section 2, deploy ONE probing phrase:

| If their answer was...                       | Use phrase                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Short / confirmed but didn't elaborate       | 🔬 #1 "Can you tell me a little bit more about that?"               |
| Vague / abstract ("yeah, kinda all of that") | 🔬 #2 "What exactly does that look like for you?"                   |
| Process-referenced ("our office handles it") | 🔬 #3 "Can you walk me through how you guys handle this currently?" |

**Optional second-tier sharpener (pick ONE if energy is high):**

- **Q1 | impact on the business:** *"When that's happening, what does that actually cost you? Like in jobs or in time?"*
- **Q2 | what they've tried:** *"Have you tried anything to fix that, or has it just kind of been the way it is?"*
- **Q3 | how they feel about it:** *"And how are you feeling about that lately? Is it getting worse, about the same, or what?"*

**Tonality reads:**

| Tone           | Adjustment                                                                                                            |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RUSHED         | Skip second-tier sharpener. Move directly to Section 4.                                                                |
| CALM (default) | Hold silence after their answer. Three to four seconds. They'll add the most important sentence in that gap.           |
| ANNOYED        | Use 🔬 #1 only (smallest ask). Skip second-tier sharpener. Move to Section 4.                                          |

**Bridge → Section 4:** *"Right, yeah."* / *"Yeah that's actually pretty common."* / *"Hmm, yeah."*

## 🎬 SECTION 4: GOALS (one or two questions, second only if energy is high)

```
A | present-tense ideal:
"If everything was running the way you wanted, what would that
look like for you?"

B | outcome and meaning:
"And what would it actually mean for the business if that got
handled?"

C | time-bounded, only if rapport is strong:
"Where do you want this to be a year from now if it got dialed in?"
```

[Wait. Don't fill silence. They're imagining their own future. Their answer here is the language used in Section 5.]

**Bridge → Section 5:** "Got it." / "Yeah that makes sense." / "Right."

## 🤝 SECTION 5: OPEN CLOSE (reference the gap in THEIR words)

```
"Well I'm not sure this is even a fit yet, that depends on what I'd
see if I actually looked at your setup. But based on what you just
said, sounds like there's a gap between [their Section 3 pain in
their words] and [their Section 4 goal in their words]. Would you
be open to a quick fifteen minutes this week to look at what's
actually causing that gap? No pitch, just want to see if I can
actually help."

[Wait. Don't refill the silence.]
```

**Example with filled-in references (illustrative, not a template):**

> "But based on what you just said, sounds like there's a gap between leads slipping through when things get busy, and you wanting to be in a spot where you stop even thinking about it. Would you be open to a quick fifteen minutes this week to look at what's actually causing that gap?"

**Tonality reads:**

| Tone           | Adjustment                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RUSHED         | Tighten: *"Real quick, would you be open to fifteen minutes this week to look at what's causing that gap? I can put it together on my time, you just tell me when."* "I do the work, you pick the time" lowers the ask. |
| CALM (default) | Hold silence. They're weighing. Don't refill. If hesitation, soften: *"Take your time, no rush."*                                                                       |
| ANNOYED        | Don't try the full close. Pivot to callback ask: *"Honestly, this doesn't sound like the right moment. Would it be alright if I followed up in a couple weeks when things are calmer?"* Get permission. That's the win. |

**Once they say yes:**

```
"Cool. What works better, later this week or early next week?"

[Let them pick.]

"Got it. I'll shoot you a text to confirm. Best number to reach you?"

[Get the number. Hang up. Do NOT keep talking.]
```

## 🔬 PROBING PHRASES (3-tool deepening library)

Three NEPQ consequence-question phrases. Each pulls a different shape of answer. Deploy after a primary question (Section 2, 3, or 4) to deepen what they just gave you. One probing phrase per primary question. Stacking two reads like an interrogation.

**1. "Can you tell me a little bit more about that?"**

- **Pulls:** breadth on a short or one-liner answer
- **When to use:** they gave a clipped answer and you sense there's more there
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Yeah that tracks. So when that happens, what's the move?"

**2. "What exactly does that look like for you?"**

- **Pulls:** specifics on a vague or abstract answer
- **When to use:** they used soft language ("kinda," "sometimes," "it's whatever," "you know how it is")
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Got it. How often does that happen?"

**3. "Can you walk me through how you guys handle this situation currently?"**

- **Pulls:** process detail when they reference a system or another person
- **When to use:** they said "our office," "we just," "my wife handles that," "the guys take care of it"
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Okay. Is that working for you, or is it more just kinda there?"

**Default rule:** one probing phrase per primary question. The probing phrase is what makes pain land. Without it, Section 3 is a survey.

## ❌ NEPQ Objection Handles

Acknowledge briefly. Ask a question that splits the objection. Let them resolve it.

### "Not interested."

```
"Yeah no, totally fair. Real quick before I let you go, what makes
you say that, is it that what you're doing now is already working
well, or is it that this kind of thing hasn't worked for you in the
past?"

[Wait. Don't defend.]

If "already working": "Got it, that's the dream. What's working best
for you right now?" (back into situation Q)
If "hasn't worked": "Yeah, most common thing I hear. What happened
the last time?"
```

> **Variant note:** the "(back into situation Q)" loop-back now lands in the PAIN HYPOTHESIS frame (Section 2 of this script), not a standalone situation question. The prospect-facing wording stays. Just route the next question through Section 2.

### "Already have someone."

```
"Got it. Are they handling all of it or just certain pieces?"

[Silence. They name the gap.]

"Yeah that tracks. And the parts they're handling, is that actually
moving the needle for you or is it more just kind of there?"
```

### "Send me info."

```
"Yeah I get that. Real quick, what would you actually want to see
in something I sent? Just so I'm not wasting your time with the
wrong stuff."

[Wait. They describe what would be useful.]

"Got it, makes sense. Honestly that's the kind of thing that's hard
to put in an email without me knowing more about your setup. Would
you be open to a quick fifteen so I can pull it together for you
specifically?"
```

### "Want to think about it."

```
"Yeah, totally. Real quick though, what specifically are you
thinking about, is it whether it'd actually help, or the timing, or
something else?"

[Wait. "Think about it" is never the real objection.]

If "whether it'd help": "Got it. What would you need to see to feel
like it would?"
If "timing": "Yeah. When does timing usually clear up for you?"
If "something else": let them name it, then question into it.
```

### "How much?"

```
"Honest answer, depends on what you actually need, and I don't know
that yet. Real quick though, before I throw a number that's
probably wrong, what would have to be true for the cost to be worth
it for you?"

[Wait. Long pause is fine.]

"Right, yeah, makes sense. That's actually what the quick fifteen
would be for, figuring out what 'worth it' looks like on your side.
Worth a look?"
```

## 🛟 Recovery Moves

Six scenarios. Acknowledge, reset tone, smaller re-engagement.

**1. Came in too hot and they're calm.**
Catch it inside 10 seconds. Slow down mid-sentence. Drop volume by 20%. *"Sorry, let me start that over."* Restart at half speed.

**2. Came in too calm and they want to move fast.**
Tighten cadence by 30%. Skip softeners. *"Yeah no, I'll be quick. One question."* Go straight to a closed-form version of the situation Q.

> **Variant note:** in V3-Pain-First, the "closed-form situation Q" becomes the RUSHED variant of Section 2 PAIN HYPOTHESIS (drop to 2 pains, ask yes/no). Same fast-track logic, just routed through the pain hypothesis frame.

**3. Stumbled on a word.**
Quick recover: *"Sorry, let me reset."* Pause one beat. Restart cleanly. Acknowledged stumbles read human. Ignored ones read anxious.

**4. They interrupt mid-question.**
Stop talking immediately. Listen all the way through. Bridge: *"Got it, yeah."* Then ask: *"Mind if I finish what I was asking? Should take 10 seconds."*

**5. One-word answer to an open question.**
Don't move on. *"Got it. What does that look like for you day to day?"* Or: *"Yeah? Tell me more about that."* "Tell me more" opens about 70% of one-word answers. If they go one-word twice in a row, the call is dead. Exit clean: *"Sounds like maybe not the right time. Appreciate you taking the call."*

**6. Long silence after a question (4+ seconds).**
Don't fill it. If it crosses 5 to 6 seconds, soften: *"Take your time, no rush."* If they still don't answer, shrink to a closed question. Trade open for closed. That's the trade you wanted.

## Why This Variant Exists

V3-Simplified opens Section 2 with an open situation question ("What are you guys doing right now to bring in new pool customers?"). That works but takes 60-90 seconds of prospect monologue before pain surfaces.

V3-Pain-First inverts this. Lead with 2-3 specific pain hypotheses based on what was found during lead enrichment. Prospect confirms (you nailed it), corrects (here's the real pain), or denies (revert to situation question fallback). Either way, you're in pain territory within 30 seconds instead of 90.

**Risks:**

- Naming wrong pains positions you as "guy who thinks he knows my business but doesn't"
- Edges closer to pitching than v3-simplified's pure-diagnostic Section 2
- Removes prospect's chance to surface unexpected pain (divorce, unhappy employee, supplier issue)

**Mitigations:**

- 7-pain library, pick 2-3 based on actual lead enrichment data (not random)
- Fallback to v3-simplified situation question if all pains denied
- Tonality reads scale pain count down for RUSHED/ANNOYED prospects

## Field Test Gate

Dial 20 pool leads with V3-Pain-First. Log:

- Connect-to-conversation rate (got past disarm into Section 2)
- Pain-hypothesis hit rate (% of times prospect confirmed one of the 2-3 pains offered)
- Pain-hypothesis correction rate (% of times prospect corrected to reveal real pain)
- Pain-hypothesis denial rate (% of times all 3 pains denied, fell back to situation Q)
- Soft-ask-to-yes rate (% of conversations that closed to discovery call)

Compare against v3-simplified baseline. If V3-Pain-First lifts soft-ask-to-yes rate by 10%+ or pain surfaces in <30 seconds in 60%+ of calls, lock V3-Pain-First as default and archive v3-simplified.
