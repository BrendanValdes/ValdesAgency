## Pre-Call Read (first 3 seconds, pick one)

1. **RUSHED:** clipped "yeah?", background noise, sigh, "make it quick"
2. **CALM:** relaxed "hello," lets silence sit, no background noise
3. **ANNOYED:** flat "what," "who is this," interruption inside 3 seconds

Default = CALM. Sections 1, 3, 5 have deviations for the other two.

```

"Have you noticed any spots where stuff isn't working as well as

D | universal feeling check:
"How are you feeling about how it's been going lately?"

E | late-in-section sharpener:
"If you could fix one thing about how you're getting customers
right now, what would it be?"
```

**STEP 2.** Deploy ONE 🔬 probing phrase to deepen the answer.

| If their Step 1 answer was...        | Use phrase                                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Short / one-liner                    | 🔬 #1 "Can you tell me a little bit more about that?"               |
| Vague / abstract ("it's whatever")   | 🔬 #2 "What exactly does that look like for you?"                   |
| Process-referenced ("our office...") | 🔬 #3 "Can you walk me through how you guys handle this currently?" |

| Tone           | Adjustment                                                                                                                                            |

| RUSHED         | Shrink Step 1 to yes/no. Skip Step 2. *"Real quick, anywhere in there leads slipping through?"* If no, pivot to callback ask.                          |

## 🔬 PROBING PHRASES (3-tool deepening library)

Three NEPQ consequence-question phrases. Each pulls a different shape of answer. Deploy after a primary question (Section 2, 3, or 4) to deepen what they just gave you. One probing phrase per primary question. Stacking two reads like an interrogation.

**1. "Can you tell me a little bit more about that?"**

- **Pulls:** breadth on a short or one-liner answer
- **When to use:** they gave a clipped answer and you sense there's more there
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Yeah that tracks. So when that happens, what's the move?"

**2. "What exactly does that look like for you?"**

- **Pulls:** specifics on a vague or abstract answer
- **When to use:** they used soft language ("kinda," "sometimes," "it's whatever," "you know how it is")
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Got it. How often does that happen?"

**3. "Can you walk me through how you guys handle this situation currently?"**

- **Pulls:** process detail when they reference a system or another person
- **When to use:** they said "our office," "we just," "my wife handles that," "the guys take care of it"
- **Follow-up after they expand:** "Okay. Is that working for you, or is it more just kinda there?"

**Default rule:** one probing phrase per primary question. The probing phrase is what makes pain land. Without it, Section 3 is a survey.

## NEPQ Objection Handles

Acknowledge briefly. Ask a question that splits the objection. Let them resolve it.

### "Not interested."

```
"Yeah no, totally fair. Real quick before I let you go, what makes
you say that, is it that what you're doing now is already working
well, or is it that this kind of thing hasn't worked for you in the
past?"

[Wait. Don't defend.]

If "already working": "Got it, that's the dream. What's working best
for you right now?" (back into situation Q)
If "hasn't worked": "Yeah, most common thing I hear. What happened
the last time?"
```

### "Already have someone."

```
"Got it. Are they handling all of it or just certain pieces?"

[Silence. They name the gap.]

"Yeah that tracks. And the parts they're handling, is that actually
moving the needle for you or is it more just kind of there?"
```

### "Send me info."

```
"Yeah I get that. Real quick, what would you actually want to see
in something I sent? Just so I'm not wasting your time with the
wrong stuff."

[Wait. They describe what would be useful.]

"Got it, makes sense. Honestly that's the kind of thing that's hard
to put in an email without me knowing more about your setup. Would
you be open to a quick fifteen so I can pull it together for you
specifically?"
```

### "Want to think about it."

```
"Yeah, totally. Real quick though, what specifically are you
thinking about, is it whether it'd actually help, or the timing, or
something else?"

[Wait. "Think about it" is never the real objection.]

If "whether it'd help": "Got it. What would you need to see to feel
like it would?"
If "timing": "Yeah. When does timing usually clear up for you?"
If "something else": let them name it, then question into it.
```

### "How much?"

```
"Honest answer, depends on what you actually need, and I don't know
that yet. Real quick though, before I throw a number that's
probably wrong, what would have to be true for the cost to be worth
it for you?"

[Wait. Long pause is fine.]

"Right, yeah, makes sense. That's actually what the quick fifteen
would be for, figuring out what 'worth it' looks like on your side.
Worth a look?"
```

## Recovery Moves

Six scenarios. Acknowledge, reset tone, smaller re-engagement.

**1. Came in too hot and they're calm.**
Catch it inside 10 seconds. Slow down mid-sentence. Drop volume by 20%. *"Sorry, let me start that over."* Restart at half speed.

**2. Came in too calm and they want to move fast.**
Tighten cadence by 30%. Skip softeners. *"Yeah no, I'll be quick. One question."* Go straight to a closed-form version of the situation Q.

**3. Stumbled on a word.**
Quick recover: *"Sorry, let me reset."* Pause one beat. Restart cleanly. Acknowledged stumbles read human. Ignored ones read anxious.

**4. They interrupt mid-question.**
Stop talking immediately. Listen all the way through. Bridge: *"Got it, yeah."* Then ask: *"Mind if I finish what I was asking? Should take 10 seconds."*

**5. One-word answer to an open question.**
Don't move on. *"Got it. What does that look like for you day to day?"* Or: *"Yeah? Tell me more about that."* "Tell me more" opens about 70% of one-word answers. If they go one-word twice in a row, the call is dead. Exit clean: *"Sounds like maybe not the right time. Appreciate you taking the call."*

**6. Long silence after a question (4+ seconds).**
Don't fill it. If it crosses 5 to 6 seconds, soften: *"Take your time, no rush."* If they still don't answer, shrink to a closed question. Trade open for closed. That's the trade you wanted.
