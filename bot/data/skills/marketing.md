- New niche question → §1 + §3
- Vegas geo question → §4
- "What channel should I use?" → §5

- "Where does this lead live in GHL?" → §9

### 1. POOL SERVICE
**Status:** 🔴 Phase 1 target — primary focus. Cross-ref `skills/sales.md` (entire file is built around this niche).
**Vegas density:** High (300+ pool service companies in metro Vegas per GBP scrape estimate; 5–20 employee shops are the sweet spot)

**ICP:** Owner-operator, 35–55 years old, often a tradesman who started solo and built to a small crew. Works in the truck or right next to it. Phone is his marketing department. Has never run a paid ad. Trusts referrals + signs at job sites. Time-poor. Fears getting scammed by an "agency" that takes his money and disappears.

**Avg deal value:**
- Recurring monthly maintenance: **$150–300/month per pool**
- One-time repairs: **$500–2,000/job**
- Equipment installs (heater, pump): **$1,500–4,000/job**
- Annual customer LTV (retained): **$1,800–3,600/year recurring + 1–2 repair calls**
- Math: 8 new customers at $200/month = $1,600/month new MRR. That covers our $1,500 retainer in month one. ROI is obvious.

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Inconsistent lead flow — slammed in summer, dead in winter
2. Owner is the sales/marketing/CEO/tech all in one
3. Burned by past marketing attempts ("paid $500, got 1 lead, never again")

**Lead-source ranking (per CLAUDE.md):**
1. Google search (high-intent: "pool service near me," "pool repair Vegas")
2. Google Local Service Ads (LSA) — pay-per-lead, Google-vetted
3. GBP organic (free if reviews are strong)
4. Meta (top of funnel — homeowner audiences)
5. Yelp (declining)
6. NextDoor (hyperlocal but slow)

**Pre-qual filters** (Review Scraper output — see §7):
- GBP rating: 3.8–4.5 (sweet spot — has reviews but room to improve)
- Review count: 20–80
- Owner response rate: <30% (means low engagement → easy positioning win)
- Site: usually exists but ugly, no clear booking flow
- Ads: rarely running anything paid

**Hook angles:**
- "Saw you're at [4.2] on Google with [38] reviews — most pool guys at that level are 1 system away from being fully booked, mind if I show you what's working in Vegas right now?"
- "Year-round pool weather here means you're leaving money on the table from October to March — most Vegas pool companies don't market off-season and that's where the easy wins are"
- "Watched [competitor name] grow from 20 to 60 pools in the last year — they're not better than you, they just have a different lead system. Want to see it?"
- "Most pool companies in Vegas wait for referrals. The ones doubling are running a 3-channel system. Got 10 minutes?"

**Pricing fit:** $1,500/month starter (with case-study trade for first 2 Vegas clients). $2,000/month growth tier once they're at capacity.

**Seasonality:**
- **Push hard:** Feb–Mar (gear up for season), Sep–Oct (post-summer regret)
- **Steady:** Apr–Aug (they're slammed — sell them off-season prep)
- **Soft sell:** Nov–Dec (slow season, more time to talk; tougher to sign)

**Top objection + handle:** (see `skills/sales.md` §1 for full library)
> Q: "I've tried marketing before, didn't work."
> A: "Yeah, most agencies run the same generic ads for HVAC, roofing, pool — they don't know the difference. I only work with pool companies. I know exactly what gets pool customers to call."

**Status notes:** No paying pool clients yet. Tyler dialing daily (target 60/day). SonoView CPL data is the proof point we drop on every call. 10-leads-in-30-days guarantee available when math works.

### 2. PEST CONTROL
**Status:** 🟡 Niche backlog — port playbook after pool client #3
**Vegas density:** High (scorpions, roaches, ants, rodents — Vegas is a year-round pest market; 200+ companies in metro)

**ICP:** Owner-operator or 5–25 employee shop. Often started as a tech with a route, now manages a small team. Recurring revenue model is the goal but most are job-by-job. Heavy reliance on door-to-door sales (especially the bigger franchises).

**Avg deal value:**
- Initial treatment: **$120–250/job**
- Quarterly recurring: **$85–150/quarter** ($340–600/year)
- Termite treatment: **$800–2,500/job**
- Annual LTV (retained): **$400–800/year**
- Math: 12 new recurring customers at $120/quarter = $1,440 quarterly = $480/month MRR. Slower payback than pool — need 30+ new customers to cover retainer in month one.

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Recurring revenue is hard to build — most customers churn after the problem is solved
2. Door-to-door sales work but require a sales team (most don't have one)
3. Vegas-specific pests (scorpions especially) drive panic buying — but they don't know how to position around it

**Lead-source ranking:**
1. Google search (urgency: "scorpion exterminator Vegas," "roaches in apartment")
2. GBP (especially with strong reviews — pest is a trust play)
3. Google LSA (vetted)
4. Meta retargeting (less effective for cold; use for repeat customers)
5. Yelp (still relevant for service businesses)
6. Door-to-door (offline, not our channel — but reference it in discovery)

**Pre-qual filters:**
- GBP rating: 3.8–4.5
- Review count: 15–60 (smaller than pool — pest reviews are harder to get)
- Owner response rate: <40%
- Site: often franchise-templated (Orkin/Terminix lookalike) → easy to differentiate
- Ads: maybe running Google but unoptimized

**Hook angles:**
- "Saw you're at [4.1] stars with [22] reviews — pest control wins on trust, and your review velocity is a lever you're not pulling. Quick fix?"
- "Vegas scorpion calls peak May–September. Most pest companies don't have a campaign built around it. Want to see what we'd do?"
- "Most pest companies sell job-by-job and lose 40% of customers after one treatment. We've got a 3-step system that doubles recurring conversion. Got 10 minutes?"
- "Termite season's coming — your competitors who'll close 20 termite jobs in May already started marketing in March. Are you ready?"

**Pricing fit:** $1,500/month starter. $2,000 growth tier for shops with 10+ techs.

**Seasonality:**
- **Push hard:** Mar–May (scorpion + termite season prep), Sep (post-summer roach surge)
- **Steady:** Jun–Aug (peak panic buying — they're slammed)
- **Soft sell:** Nov–Feb (slow indoor pests — tougher conversation)

**Top objection + handle:**
> Q: "Pest is different — most leads need same-day service, marketing leads come in too slow."
> A: "Right — that's why we set up Local Service Ads + emergency keyword campaigns first. Most pest leads close within 4 hours of the first call. We don't run slow-burn campaigns for pest. We run urgency."

**Status notes:** Not active. First port target after pool playbook proven. Vegas-scorpion angle is a free differentiator.

### 3. GARAGE DOOR
**Status:** 🟡 Niche backlog — high-urgency, high-AOV, easy lead capture
**Vegas density:** Medium-High (~80–120 companies in metro; lots of solo operators)

**ICP:** Usually solo owner-operator or 1–3 truck shop. Repair-heavy (replacement of springs, openers, panels) with occasional new-door installs. High urgency = high willingness to pay for fast lead delivery.

**Avg deal value:**
- Spring repair: **$150–400/job**
- Opener replacement: **$300–600/job**
- New door install: **$1,500–4,500/job**
- No real recurring revenue
- Annual LTV: **$300–600/year** (occasional repeat + referral)
- Math: 6 new install customers at $2k = $12k revenue, retainer is paid 8x over.

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Cyclical demand — heavy after storms / seasonal swings
2. Competition with national chains (A1, Precision) that dominate Google Ads
3. Lead leakage — they get a call, they're on a job, they miss it, they lose it

**Lead-source ranking:**
1. Google search (highest intent — "garage door repair near me" is gold)
2. Google LSA (massive for this category — Google trust signal matters)
3. GBP (urgency searches go GBP-first)
4. Yelp (still holds for emergency services)
5. Meta (low intent — only useful for installs, not repairs)
6. Direct mail / NextDoor (long tail)

**Pre-qual filters:**
- GBP rating: 4.0–4.7 (garage door reviews are strict — anything below 4 is suspect to customers)
- Review count: 20–100
- Owner response rate: <30%
- Site: often a 2010-era template
- Ads: probably running Google with bad QS, getting outspent by chains

**Hook angles:**
- "Garage door repairs are the highest-intent searches in home services — I noticed you're at [4.3] stars but only on page 2 of 'garage door repair Vegas.' That's leaving 60% of clicks on the table. 10 minutes?"
- "Precision and A1 are running $80 CPCs in Vegas. We get our garage door clients down to $35 by going local-only. Want to see how?"
- "Spring season + storm season = your busiest months. Most independent garage door shops aren't ready. Let's get your bookings doubled by April."
- "I noticed you're missing call tracking on your site — every missed call in this niche is a $400 job lost. Easy fix?"

**Pricing fit:** $1,500/month — the per-lead value is so high they pay for the retainer in 2–3 closed jobs.

**Seasonality:**
- **Push hard:** Year-round but especially Feb–Apr (spring breakage cycle) + Sep–Nov (post-summer storms)
- **Soft sell:** Dec–Jan (people delay garage repairs for the holidays)

**Top objection + handle:**
> Q: "I just need more calls — not a marketing system."
> A: "Same thing. Calls don't come from luck — they come from showing up first on Google when someone's broke-down door is opening. We get you there in 30 days. Want the playbook?"

**Status notes:** Not active. Strong candidate for fast wins — niche has high AOV, high urgency, easy attribution.

### 4. HVAC
**Status:** 🟡 Niche backlog — biggest budgets, biggest LTV, biggest competition
**Vegas density:** Very High (Vegas heat = 250+ HVAC companies; the market is saturated and sophisticated)

**ICP:** Multi-truck operations (10–50 employees common). More sophisticated than pool/pest — many already work with marketing agencies. Owner profile splits: tradesman-founders vs. private-equity-rolled-up shops. The PE-rolled shops are not our buyer (they have CMOs).

**Avg deal value:**
- Service call: **$100–250/job**
- Maintenance plan: **$200–400/year recurring**
- Repair: **$300–1,500/job**
- New AC install: **$5,000–15,000/job**
- Full system replacement: **$8,000–20,000/job**
- Annual customer LTV (retained): **$300–500/year + every 8–12 years a $10k install**
- Math: 1 install close = $10k revenue. Retainer is paid 6x over by ONE close.

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Marketing is crowded and expensive — Google CPCs in Vegas can hit $80+ for "AC repair"
2. Lead quality varies wildly — they pay for "leads" that don't convert
3. Seasonality is brutal — summer = chaos, winter = cricket sounds

**Lead-source ranking:**
1. Google LSA (Google's verified pro program — HVAC is the prime category)
2. Google search (high-intent emergency repair terms)
3. GBP (must be 4.5+ to compete in Vegas)
4. Meta (works for maintenance plan upsells, not cold)
5. Direct mail (still works for established shops)
6. Truck wraps (offline brand, not our channel)

**Pre-qual filters:**
- GBP rating: 4.3+ (anything below = won't buy our service, they need ops fix first)
- Review count: 50–500 (we want established but not huge)
- Owner response rate: any (HVAC owners are too busy to reply)
- Employee count: 10–30 (above 30 = probably has CMO; below 10 = probably can't afford $2k retainer)
- Ads: usually running Google + LSA, probably overspending

**Hook angles:**
- "Most Vegas HVAC shops are bleeding 30% of their Google budget on terms that don't convert. Quick audit, no pitch — interested?"
- "Maintenance plans are the LTV play — your installs print money but your maintenance plan conversion is probably under 20%. We get clients to 45%. Want to see how?"
- "Summer's coming. Your Google CPC will double in May. Want to lock in May–August spend on a fixed-cost plan now?"
- "Saw you're spending on LSA but your GBP isn't fully optimized — that's like running ads to a broken landing page. 15-minute fix."

**Pricing fit:** **$2,000/month minimum.** HVAC requires more sophisticated work (LSA mgmt, GBP optimization, maintenance plan funnel) — don't undersell.

**Seasonality:**
- **Push hard:** Mar–Apr (pre-summer prep — owners panic about summer readiness)
- **Slow:** Jun–Aug (they're slammed, won't take meetings)
- **Push again:** Sep–Oct (post-summer reflection)
- **Slow:** Nov–Feb (zero urgency)

**Top objection + handle:**
> Q: "We already work with [HVAC marketing agency name]."
> A: "Cool — most Vegas HVAC shops do. What's your average CPL right now? [listen]. If you're paying more than $50, there's room. If under, we don't need to talk. Want to compare?"

**Status notes:** Not active. Highest-revenue niche per client but also hardest to penetrate. Wait until pool playbook is proven before going here.

### 5. LANDSCAPING

**Vegas density:** High (200+ shops; mix of full-service and lawn-only operators)

**ICP:** Mostly owner-operator with 2–10 employees. Two flavors: maintenance (mow/edge/blow recurring) and design-build (one-time projects, $5k–$50k). Different buying profiles.

**Avg deal value:**
- Recurring maintenance: **$80–250/month per residential**
- Commercial maintenance: **$500–3,000/month per property**
- Design-build project: **$3,000–25,000/job**
- Annual LTV (retained residential): **$1,200–3,000/year**

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Race-to-the-bottom pricing on maintenance ($60/mow guys are everywhere)
2. Project work is unpredictable — feast or famine
3. Hard to differentiate — most look identical online

**Lead-source ranking:**
1. Google search (project work — "landscape design Las Vegas")
2. GBP (maintenance, especially with photos)
3. NextDoor (hyperlocal, surprisingly strong)
4. Referrals (still #1 in this niche)
5. Meta (works with strong before/after photos)
6. Yelp (declining)

**Pre-qual filters:**
- GBP rating: 4.0–4.6
- Review count: 15–80
- Photo count: <50 (means they're not maximizing GBP — easy win)
- Site: often ugly with bad photos
- Ads: rarely running paid

**Hook angles:**
- "Design-build is your highest margin work but most landscape companies in Vegas only market for maintenance. We flip that ratio. Got 10 minutes?"
- "Your GBP has [12] photos — companies winning landscape work in Vegas have 200+. That's a free fix worth 30% more leads."
- "Most landscape companies live and die by referrals. We layer Google + NextDoor on top so when winter hits, you're not panicking."
- "Spring's the busiest landscape season in Vegas. The shops that book up by March started marketing in January. Where are you?"

**Pricing fit:** $1,500/month for maintenance-focused; $2,000/month if going after design-build.

**Seasonality:**
- **Push hard:** Jan–Mar (spring prep), Sep–Oct (fall cleanup + winter prep)
- **Steady:** Apr–Aug (busy but workable)
- **Slow sell:** Nov–Dec (they're not buying)

**Top objection + handle:**

> A: "Then your foundation is solid. We're not replacing referrals — we're adding a second engine so when referrals slow in October, you're still booked. Most of our landscape clients come to us BECAUSE they have great referrals. Adding paid is the next layer."

**Status notes:** Not active. Less urgent than pest/garage/HVAC. Comes after.

### 7. HOUSE CLEANING

- Move-in/out: **$300–800/job**
- Annual LTV (retained): **$3,600–8,000/year per recurring customer** — STRONG

**Top 3 pain points:**
1. Hiring/retaining cleaners (turnover destroys ops)
2. Customer churn after the "honeymoon" period
3. Pricing pressure from franchise chains (Molly Maid, Merry Maids)

**Lead-source ranking:**
1. Google search (recurring intent: "weekly house cleaning Vegas")
2. GBP (reviews matter enormously)
3. NextDoor (very strong)
4. Meta (works for recurring service offers)
5. Referral programs (huge in this niche)
6. Yelp (still useful)

**Pre-qual filters:**
- GBP rating: 4.4+
- Review count: 30+
- Recurring customer count: 20+ (means they have ops chops)
- Site: most have a quote form
- Ads: usually not running anything systematic

**Hook angles:**
- "Recurring is the only game in house cleaning — you're at [25%] recurring? Most Vegas shops are. We get clients to 60%."
- "Your churn after month 3 is probably killing your CAC math. Quick fix is in the onboarding flow, not the marketing."
- "Most cleaning companies treat one-time deep cleans as the goal. They're actually the LEAD MAGNET — let me show you the funnel."
- "Vegas has a massive Airbnb cleaning market. Are you in it? Most local companies aren't and it's the easiest recurring revenue in the niche."

**Pricing fit:** $1,500/month — if they have 20+ recurring customers, the math works.

**Seasonality:**
- **Steady year-round** with bumps for: pre-holidays (Nov–Dec deep cleans), post-holidays (Jan reset cleans), spring cleaning (Apr–May)

**Top objection + handle:**
> Q: "Marketing isn't the problem — finding cleaners is the problem."

**Status notes:** Not active. Strong fit but volume of competition is high. Airbnb cleaning sub-segment is a wedge worth exploring.

## §7 — REVIEW SCRAPER WORKFLOW (SOP)

**Goal:** Every cold email touch #1 contains a personalization line that proves we looked the prospect up. This single line drives reply rate from ~1% (generic) to ~5% (personalized).

### Tools

- **Apify** — Google Maps Reviews scraper actor (primary) + Yelp scraper actor (secondary)
- **Apollo.io** — to pull lead lists with GMB URLs attached
- **Google Sheets** — to enrich + merge data
- **GHL** — to import contacts with custom fields + send sequences
- (Optional) **Brave Search MCP** — for verification when GMB data is incomplete

### Data captured per prospect

| Field | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Apollo / GMB | Personalization |
| GMB URL | Apollo / Brave | Reference |
| Address | GMB | Geo-targeting validation |
| Owner first name | Apollo | `{{owner_first_name}}` |
| Star rating (current) | Apify | `{{rating}}` |
| Review count | Apify | `{{review_count}}` |
| Date of last review | Apify | Freshness check |
| Top 3 negative themes (last 20 reviews) | Apify + manual tagging | `{{negative_theme}}` |
| Owner response rate (% reviews replied to) | Apify | `{{response_gap}}` |
| Last owner response date | Apify | Engagement signal |

### Personalization tokens (for cold email merge)

- `{{rating}}` → "saw your 4.2 rating on Google"
- `{{review_count}}` → "with 38 reviews"
- `{{negative_theme}}` → "noticed a few customers mentioned scheduling delays"
- `{{response_gap}}` → "haven't seen any owner replies in the last 60 days — that's an easy win"
- `{{competitor_name}}` → optional, when manual research surfaces a clear local competitor

### Pre-qual cutoffs (skip rules)

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Rating < 3.8 | SKIP — too risky, they have ops issues we can't fix |
| Rating > 4.7 AND review count > 100 | SKIP — probably already has marketing handled |
| Review count < 15 | SKIP — not enough surface area for personalization |
| No website found | SKIP — can't deliver our service without a site to start from |
| GBP marked as "permanently closed" | SKIP — obvious |

**Sweet spot:** **3.8–4.5 stars, 20–80 reviews, owner response rate < 30%**.

### SOP — step by step

**Step 1: Build the lead list**
- Apollo.io filter: industry = pool service (or target niche), location = Las Vegas metro (40-mile radius), employee count = 5–25, has phone number.
- Export with: company name, owner name, owner email, owner LinkedIn URL, GMB URL.
- Target: 100 leads per batch.

**Step 2: Run Apify scraper**
- Open Apify → Google Maps Reviews scraper actor.
- Input: list of GMB URLs from Step 1.
- Run with: max 20 reviews per business, include owner responses, include review dates.
- Output: JSON or CSV.

**Step 3: Enrich in Google Sheets**
- Import Apify CSV.
- Add columns: rating, review count, last review date, response rate (% of reviews with owner reply), last response date.
- Manually tag top 3 negative themes per business (look at last 20 reviews — common themes: scheduling, communication, pricing, quality).
- Apply pre-qual cutoffs — delete rows that fail.
- Target: ~50–60 qualified leads from a 100-lead batch.

**Step 4: Import to GHL**
- Add custom fields in GHL: `rating`, `review_count`, `negative_theme`, `response_gap`, `gmb_url`.
- Import contacts with all merge fields populated.
- Tag: `cold-pool-{{batch-date}}` (or other niche).

**Step 5: Launch sequence**
- Trigger Touch 1 of the 5-touch cold email sequence (§6).
- Tyler watches GHL daily for replies.
- Positive replies → Tyler calls within 2 hours.

### Quality control

- Spot-check 5 emails per batch before launch — if `{{negative_theme}}` is missing, fix manually.
- Don't send if any merge field is blank (GHL has a "skip if missing" rule — use it).
- Track reply rate per batch. If a batch underperforms 3% reply rate, retire that hook angle.

### Refresh cadence

Re-scrape reviews quarterly per active outreach niche — ratings drift, response rates change, new negative themes emerge.

### Positioning rules

1. **Lead with their pain in their words.** Not "we provide marketing services" — "you're slammed in summer, dead in winter, and tired of paying for marketing that doesn't work."
2. **Single-niche positioning beats generalist.** "I only work with pool companies" > "I help service businesses grow."
3. **Vegas-local is a real moat.** Most agencies are remote / national. Local knowledge of zip codes, seasonality, neighborhoods is differentiator.
4. **Numbers as credibility.** Never "we get great results." Always "$20–35 CPL on Google for our SonoView client."
5. **Guarantee when the math works.** "10 leads in 30 days or you don't pay month two" only when (a) the market exists, (b) the landing page will be done right, (c) the niche economics support the math.

## §11 — COUNCIL LCM — APPLIED EXAMPLES

Council LCM = ROCCO's 5-lens decision framework (see CLAUDE.md). For any strategy / pricing / clients / growth / money question, run all 5 lenses BEFORE recommending.

The 5 lenses:
- **M — Marketing:** Does the market care?
- **R — Revenue:** Does this make money?
- **O — Operations:** Can we actually execute?
- **S — Strategy:** Is this the right move long-term?
- **F — Finance:** What do the numbers say?

### Worked example 1: "Should we expand to HVAC or stick to pool?"

**M — Marketing:** HVAC is bigger budget per client, but more sophisticated buyers. Pool is less crowded, easier hook (year-round Vegas weather). **Pool wins on hook quality; HVAC wins on size.**

**R — Revenue:** Pool retainer = $1.5–2k/mo. HVAC retainer = $2k+/mo, plus higher LTV per client (HVAC clients churn less because their installs are bigger). **HVAC wins on per-client revenue.**

**O — Operations:** We have ZERO HVAC playbook. Building one would take 30+ hours of research + 2–3 weeks of cold dialing to learn the niche. Pool playbook is LIVE. **Pool wins on operational readiness.**

**S — Strategy:** Long-term, niche depth beats niche width. Owning "Vegas pool service marketing" is a defensible moat. Spreading across 8 niches dilutes positioning. **Pool wins on strategic depth.**

**F — Finance:** Pool client #1 is more cash-flow-positive in 30 days than HVAC client #1 (which would take 60+ days to even land). Cash matters now. **Pool wins on cash math.**

**Verdict:** Stick to pool. **5 of 5 lenses say pool.** Don't expand to HVAC until pool has 3 paying clients (per CLAUDE.md Phase 1 → 2 logic). Conflict that surfaced: HVAC has bigger long-term revenue, but every other lens says now is wrong. Reassess in 6 months.

### Worked example 2: "Should we drop $1,500 to $1,200 to close faster?"

**M — Marketing:** A lower price doesn't actually move pool service owners — they're more sensitive to "will this work" than "what does it cost." Dropping price suggests we don't believe in the result. **Marketing says NO.**

**R — Revenue:** $1,200/mo × 12 = $14,400/year per client. $1,500/mo × 12 = $18,000/year. Per client, dropping to $1,200 costs us $3,600/year. Even if it closes 20% faster, we don't gain. **Revenue says NO.**

**O — Operations:** Same ops cost either way (we're delivering the same work). Lower margin = less budget for tools, less ability to absorb learning-phase ad spend. **Ops says NO.**

**S — Strategy:** Once you set a price, raising it later is hard. Starting at $1,200 anchors us as a budget agency. Strategy says we should hold $1,500 and let some prospects walk. **Strategy says NO.**

**F — Finance:** At $1,500/mo, we need 4 clients to hit $6k/mo. At $1,200, we need 5. The marginal cost of acquiring client #5 (extra dials, extra time) is HIGHER than the gain. **Finance says NO.**

**Verdict:** Hold at $1,500. **5 of 5 lenses say NO.** If a specific deal is stuck on price, offer setup-fee waiver or a 90-day case-study trade — but don't drop the retainer. Conflict that surfaced: zero. This one's clean.
